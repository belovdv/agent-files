# Literature and alternatives

## Map the landscape

Translate the user's question into mechanisms, alternative terminology, and relevant neighboring fields. Preserve their actual proposal and constraints; do not replace an unfamiliar idea with a simpler familiar one before searching.

Search primary papers, official documentation, implementations, and evaluations. Follow references backward and useful later work forward. Match versions when comparing a paper with its implementation. Record enough search scope, selection criteria, and dates to explain coverage and support later updates; a full systematic review is unnecessary unless requested.

Organize findings by approach and assumptions, not by discovery order. Distinguish established results, preliminary evidence, and speculative combinations. Inspect the workload and evaluation behind performance or quality claims. Do not infer novelty or nonexistence from a few unsuccessful searches.

## Compare for the actual use case

Identify mandatory semantics, workload, resource constraints, and priorities before ranking candidates. Filter incompatible options first, documenting why. Include the current approach as a baseline.

Separate algorithmic capability, implemented support, default configuration, and integration effort. A poor default is not proof that the underlying algorithm is unsuitable. Compare published numbers only when their conditions make the comparison meaningful; otherwise state what can and cannot be inferred.

Use a decision table when helpful: candidate, required capabilities, evidence, limitations, adaptation needed, and likely benefit. Rank usefulness and effort relative to the user's goals, with uncertainty. Include conditional recommendations and what evidence would change them; do not force a single winner when workload differences dominate.

A literature comparison does not itself authorize benchmarking or implementation. For an explicitly analysis-only request, propose the discriminating experiment instead of running it.

Methodological basis: adapt the explicit questions, source selection, evidence mapping, and limitations in [PRISMA scoping-review guidance](https://www.prisma-statement.org/scoping) proportionately to the task.
